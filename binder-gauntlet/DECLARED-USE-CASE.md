# Declared use case — computational design and in-silico screening of protein binders

> The program reviews an organisation's traffic against its declared use case, so the statement must be accurate for *your* organisation. Adapt the bracketed parts; do not widen it to cover work you are not doing.

## Statement

> **Use case: computational design and in-silico screening of de novo protein binders against named targets in our discovery portfolio (Drug and countermeasure discovery and preclinical support; Bioinformatics, computational biology, and research data infrastructure).**
>
> Protein engineers, computational biologists and structural biologists in [organisation / department], approximately [n] named staff, will use the program's model, from Claude Code, to drive open-source protein-design and structure-prediction software on compute we control [a Kubernetes cluster with GPU nodes in our own tenant / an on-premises Slurm cluster / GPU workstations], in interactive sessions in which the GPU steps run as batch jobs on that compute. In each session the model reads experimental structures of a target protein domain, proposes a binding site with a stated rationale, and runs backbone-generation and sequence-design tools to produce candidate miniprotein binders of roughly 50 to 120 residues. It then refolds each candidate with structure-prediction programs, filters and clusters the candidates on the programs' interface confidence and on agreement with the design model, runs the same screen on public control sequences, profiles each design's sequence liabilities and surface properties against public reference proteins, and writes a panel of designs with per-design scores, a validation and developability dossier, and a report of what was done and what was not checked. Targets are [human and model-organism proteins in our therapeutic areas, e.g. immune-checkpoint receptors, cytokines and their receptors, enzymes]; inputs are public Protein Data Bank entries and UniProt records and, later, our own unpublished structures of the same kinds of target.
>
> Outputs are computational predictions only. A protein scientist reviews every panel, including sequence searches against known proteins and a check with a structure predictor not used in the session, before any sequence is ordered, expressed or tested; binding is established only by our own or a contract laboratory's assays, never by the model's scores. Results feed internal design reviews and the choice of constructs for expression.
>
> **We will not** design binders, inhibitors or mimics for toxins, for proteins of human, animal or plant pathogens, or for any agent or activity that our institution handles under enhanced containment; we will not ask for modifications intended to increase the toxicity, transmissibility or host range of anything; we will not submit patient-derived sequences, protected health information or customer-level clinical data; and we will not use the grant for work outside the categories above. Expected volume is [a few] sessions per week of one to eight hours each, occasionally a day-long session on several GPUs, by the named staff only.

## How to adapt it

- Replace every bracketed item. Name the target classes you actually work on; a reviewer reads "named targets in our discovery portfolio" together with that list.
- If your first runs use only the public sample target in this guide, you can add: "Initial evaluation uses public PD-L1 structures that the guide's sample script fetches from the Protein Data Bank."
- Say where the compute runs (your own cloud tenant, on premises) and that the GPU steps are batch jobs there; it helps a reviewer understand why the traffic is long tool-driving sessions with job scripts going out and large result tables coming back.
- If a contract research organisation will express and assay the designs, you may say so in the results paragraph; do not name it.
- If you also want the model to help plan the expression constructs or the binding assay, that fits under this statement's results paragraph only as far as planning goes; reviewing assay *data* and designing the next round is the *design-make-test-loop* library entry and is better declared when you get there.
- Do not add unrelated activities (antibody humanisation, clinical protocol review, literature mining) to this statement to "cover" them; declare them as their own use case. Your organisation can hold more than one: each grant is one team and its declared use case (up to 100 seats), and your admin adds another use case in the Verification Portal, where it is reviewed like the first. Outside Claude Science and the Console only your organisation's default grant applies today.

## Access level

This use case is expected to fall under the program's **Standard Use** grant, and the category names in the statement are the program's published ones. The program's launch post names basic science and R&D among the areas Standard Use is designed for, and "Drug and countermeasure discovery and preclinical support" is a published category; whether your own work is in scope is decided by the use case your organisation declared and had approved, not by this guide. Ask your program contact if unsure which category to file under. The exclusions paragraph is what keeps it there: binder design against toxins or pathogen proteins is the kind of project the program scopes under its High-risk Use add-on, if it permits it at all, and must be declared separately for a named project before any such work starts.
