# Writing Style

Write for a clinical-development reader: present the facts clearly and
completely.

## Inline citation format

Cite every factual claim with an inline markdown link:
`[descriptive claim text](source_url)`. Write every link as `[title](url)`;
when there is no natural claim text, use the source name and document type
as the title.

One claim per link: each number, date or status carries the link of the source
that states it, and the linked words say no more than that source does. When a
sentence combines facts from several sources, link each fact separately. Write
an inference or calculation of your own as your own ("this implies…", "by our
arithmetic…"), outside the link and not straight after it.

Use numbered references `[1]` only when the same source is cited 5+ times or
when citing several sources for one claim.

## What to cite

Always cite: quantitative data (prevalence and incidence, patient numbers,
efficacy metrics) · clinical endpoints and results · dates (approvals, trial
completion estimates) · safety data.

Don't cite: general medical knowledge, your own analytical interpretations,
or calculations you perform from cited inputs (cite the inputs).

## Link specificity

Link to the exact page, never a homepage.

- ClinicalTrials.gov: `https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT########`
- PubMed: `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/<PMID>/` (prefer PMID over DOI redirect)
- Company: the specific press release or presentation URL

## Conflicting sources

When sources disagree, cite both and state which one you use and why:

> The press release reports [200 patients](https://sponsor.example/news/topline-results) while ClinicalTrials.gov
> shows [180 enrolled](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00000000). The registry figure of 180 is used.

## Before finalizing

Confirm every quantitative claim, trial registry ID, date, and efficacy
figure carries an inline link to a specific (not homepage) URL, and that
conflicting sources are acknowledged. Do not write "studies show" without
naming the studies; do not backfill citations after writing.
